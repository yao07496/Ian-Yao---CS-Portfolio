# Holistic Signature Work Reflection

Throughout my time at the University of St. Thomas, I have grown not only as a computer science student, but also as a person who better understands the relationship between technology, collaboration, and the common good. When I first entered computer science, I primarily viewed the field as coding and technical problem solving. I believed success in technology mainly depended on programming ability and technical knowledge. However, through interdisciplinary experiences, technical projects, healthcare exposure, and collaborative learning, my perspective has evolved significantly. I now understand that technology is most meaningful when it is designed to improve people’s lives, increase accessibility, and solve real-world problems responsibly.

One issue that strongly connects to both my academic experiences and the common good is accessibility to technology and information systems. This theme became increasingly important to me through both my coursework and professional experiences in healthcare research technology. Through computer science, healthcare technology, and interdisciplinary collaboration, I learned that technology is not only about creating software, but also about creating systems that are usable, reliable, ethical, and accessible for diverse groups of people.

One of the most important experiences that shaped my understanding of this issue was working on the Minnesota Alliance on Crime Resource Web Application project. This project combined computer science, user-centered design, and social impact. The goal of the project was to create a centralized platform where users could locate crime prevention and community support resources throughout Minnesota. From a technical perspective, the project involved frontend development, filtering systems, interactive map functionality, database integration, and user interface design using technologies such as Vue.js, JavaScript, SQL, and Leaflet. However, beyond the technical implementation, the project taught me the importance of accessibility and usability in technology design.

During development, our team faced several technical challenges involving filtering systems and real-time map updates. Some features initially failed to update correctly, which negatively impacted the user experience. My role involved debugging frontend filtering logic, improving responsiveness, and helping organize the layout to make the application more intuitive for users. This experience helped me strengthen my technical problem-solving abilities, but it also taught me how important communication and teamwork are in collaborative software development. Through this project, I realized that even well-designed technical systems can fail to serve users effectively if usability and accessibility are not prioritized.

Another major learning experience came from my Interactive Creative Coding Final Project using JavaScript and P5.js. This project emphasized creativity, interaction, and user engagement through dynamic graphical systems. While developing the project, I encountered several issues related to animation timing and event handling. At first, I focused too heavily on implementing new features instead of ensuring the core systems were stable and functional. As a result, some components became difficult to debug and maintain.

This became one of my most valuable “failure to learning” experiences. I learned that rushing to add complexity without fully testing foundational systems often creates larger problems later in development. Through debugging and restructuring the project, I developed a more organized and iterative approach to software development. I now prioritize testing, incremental progress, and maintainable code structure much more than I did previously. This experience strengthened both my technical abilities and my resilience when facing challenges. It also showed me that failure can be an important part of growth when approached with reflection and adaptability.

In addition to technical coursework, my experiences working with healthcare research technology and REDCap administration significantly expanded my interdisciplinary perspective. Working in healthcare technology environments exposed me to the importance of reliability, communication, and ethical responsibility in information systems. Supporting research infrastructure, onboarding processes, authentication systems, and user support demonstrated how technology directly affects people’s ability to perform meaningful work in healthcare and research settings.

This experience connected computer science with healthcare administration and communication. I learned that technical knowledge alone is not enough when supporting systems used by researchers, healthcare professionals, and administrative staff. Effective communication, patience, and adaptability are equally important. Through these responsibilities, I developed stronger interpersonal skills and a better understanding of how technology must support real human needs rather than simply functioning correctly from a technical perspective.

My exposure to healthcare technology also influenced how I think about the role of technology in society. Before college, I often viewed technology primarily as innovation and efficiency. Now, I understand that technology also involves ethical considerations, accessibility, equity, and responsibility. Systems that are poorly designed or difficult to access can unintentionally create barriers for users. As a result, I have become much more interested in human-centered computing and designing systems that prioritize usability and inclusiveness.

The University of St. Thomas has played a major role in shaping these perspectives. The interdisciplinary nature of my education encouraged me to connect computer science with communication, healthcare, ethics, and social impact. Rather than viewing technical and non-technical skills separately, I now see them as deeply connected. Many of my most valuable learning experiences involved both technical problem solving and collaboration with others. Projects became more successful when communication, adaptability, and teamwork were combined with technical ability.

My experiences also helped prepare me for professional environments beyond graduation. Through group projects, technical development, healthcare technology exposure, and collaborative coursework, I became more confident in my ability to contribute to interdisciplinary teams. I learned how to explain technical concepts more clearly, collaborate with people from different backgrounds, and approach problems with both analytical and human-centered thinking.

Looking forward, I plan to continue developing both my technical and interpersonal skills throughout my career. I hope to work in areas where technology and healthcare intersect, allowing me to contribute to systems that improve accessibility, efficiency, and patient or user experiences. I also want to continue improving my leadership, communication, and problem-solving abilities through collaborative work and lifelong learning.

Overall, my time at St. Thomas transformed my understanding of computer science and professional growth. I entered college primarily focused on technical achievement, but I now recognize that the most impactful technology combines strong technical foundations with empathy, collaboration, and ethical responsibility. Through both successes and challenges, I developed a stronger understanding of how technology can contribute to the common good and how I can continue using my skills to make meaningful contributions in the future.
